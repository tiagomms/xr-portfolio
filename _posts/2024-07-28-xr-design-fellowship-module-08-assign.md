## Module 08 Assignment - Museum Experience: The 50th Anniversary of the Portuguese Revolution in the eyes of teenagers using Unity, Blender & GenAI


{% include gallery id="gallery-students-work" caption="Experience developed with students in VR using Meta Quest 2 and 3" %}

You may access the [project's repo here](https://github.com/tiagomms/xr-bienal-coimbra-2024) or you can actually [test the project build by downloading this folder](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1Aj5XmXlMycrWPSCeLwS6ia7tanMFYijB?usp=drive_link) into your Meta Quest. 

I'm writing this blog two months after the project was completed, so these are mostly recollections.

The project took approximately 2 months from start to finish, working after hours as lead developer, project manager, educator and mentor for the students.

### Tools
- Sketchfab - 3D model repo used by teenagers
- Blender - reducing poly count, change pivot points 
- Unity 2022.3.16f1 - main design & development tool
- [Meshy](https://app.meshy.ai/)
- [3D CSM](https://www.csm.ai/)
- [HyperHuman](https://hyperhuman.deemos.com/)
- Bunch of free image generators tools to create skyboxes: [ManyWorlds.run](https://www.manyworlds.run/), [lexica.art](https://lexica.art/), [Adobe Firefly](https://firefly.adobe.com/)

### Project build versions & hardware
- Meta Quest 2 & 3
- Unity 2022.3.16f1
- Meta XR SDK All-in-one 63.0.0
- Unity XR Toolkit 2.5.2

## Challenge

The initial goal of this assignment was to create a XR Prototype for a meditation app using GenAI tools for image and 3d model generation.

At the time, TUMO Coimbra (the school where I do workshops for teenagers on Game Development as a part-time job) had received a challenge from our [hometown's Biennal](https://ofantasmadaliberdade.anozero-bienaldecoimbra.pt/en). The Biennal wanted our students to share their visions of what freedom meant for them in celebration to the 50 years of the portuguese revolution. 

> :memo: **Note:** I have already written an article about TUMO and what we do [in a previous post](../xr-design-fellowship-module-03-pre-assign/) - in which I did an homage to it using ShapesXR!

Each workshop area tried to contribute as much as it could. The team behind graphic design helped students write a flyers and big banners reminiscing the day. Animation did some computer animations related to the topic. The programming team did a wordle in words related to freedom. Cinema made short films/documentaries. Music made some freestyle activist rap. Photography took black and white photos of portraits and pictures of the city in allusion to the University student revolution in the late 60s. 

>I asked both TUMO and XR Bootcamp (and they approved) if I could merge both challenges - and make a small XR experience on the topic of democracy and freedom for the teenagers to work. And, additionally use GenAI tools to help with some 3d models (we had no 3d artists in the project). They both approved.

For most of my students, it was the first time they were using VR headsets and understand how all works. And for all of them, it was their first "full Unity project" in which they actually had to build and deliver a result in Game Development. 

{% include gallery id="gallery-biennal" caption="Biennal exhibition within TUMO Coimbra and Students working for it" %}

My main challenge was to develop the experience, document, mentor, support, project manage and then aggregate it from so many different visions, all while working full-time at TUMO and Deloitte. I am really proud of what we accomplished.

We were only able to deliver the project one month after the original deadline but the overall feedback from the students was that they loved working for something like this. 

### Breakdown of the main challenges

> The main goal was for people to experience what could have been like in Portugal dictatorial times: the feeling of being forced to be trapped in a little prison space due to your beliefs. But in the end, memories and hope would bring you resolve to go higher and further with your imagination.

So when merging both Bienal and XR Design Fellowship challenges, it all started with a dream: Being a political prisoner in Portugal late 60's/early 70's. In my prison cell, I would grab objects that would take me into places of what freedom meant for me and others before. But I wouldn't be able to leave my cell area. 

The trapping feeling should have been easy because Meta Quest's guardian room area could physically simulate a portuguese prison size (3m by 2.5m, approximately 10ft by 8.2ft). I didn't have to code the feeling of being trapped in a the space, the bars would automatically appear.

With the play area being a fixed size, I wouldn't need to design the experience to take into account other sizes. So I gather up a prefab of a cage and students would use it to understand how much they could travel within the unity scene.

But to go higher and further, I developed Portals and reused Unity XR Toolkit ladders and climbing walls to allow people to go further and even lose sense they were in a digital cage.


> The second goal was: I wanted the experience to be for everyone in a museum setting with as little setup as needed. Put the headset, walk, grab objects with their own hands (through hand tracking) and try it out. 

No Physics around the user to avoid major motion sickness, basic hand tracking and grabbing was provided by the toolkits on grabbable objects. Walking within guardian space without fancy commands or teleports should have been done with just the headset position in relation to the guardian. 

And most importantly, **when a person removed the headset, it should just restart for the next person**.

**For these reasons, a calibration would always be needed when starting the experience, so that the guardian's play area matched the space accordingly**. And a small tutorial: to grab an object --> go through a portal to understand the logistics of the experience. 

{% include video id="993956054" provider="vimeo" %}

These were my first tasks as solo developer (in order to understand the feasibility of the project) and they were tougher than expected (see next chapter).


> The third goal was: teenagers with basic Unity experience (but without coding experience) should be able to design their levels through prefabs and model imports. They would do level design of memories/dreams. Since they worked in different times and days of the week, they didn't need to match or tell a story.

This was tough in many ways as:

- Ideally, teenagers would work mostly for themselves and in groups of 2/3. I wrote in portuguese a GDD and a session-by-session script to introduce and let them to practical exercises learn what I thought they needed to learn. But most students in that age are not used to spending that much time on a problem and read documentation (in hindsight, makes total sense). So, I had to be physically present more than I wished.

- They still hadn't learned coding and not much of Unity Events. So the entire development had to be as close to drag & drop without worry as possible.

- TUMO has iMacs, no Windows. The Meta XR Simulator for Mac hadn't come out yet ([it was a hugeee pain](https://developer.oculus.com/downloads/package/meta-xr-simulator/)) so, I had to use Unity XR Toolkit and not Meta Quest building blocks.
  - For the issue of restarting the experience when taking out the headset, Unity XR Toolkit didn't work that well and there was a lack of documentation on this. So I had to merge the Oculus OVR Manager with Unity's XR Origin and remove frictions between them.  

- We had a hell of permission installation issues at the school due to not being able to build Android apks in the school Macs (it appears you need to set the student profile to admin and reinstall Unity Hub and Unity versions for it to work).

- No git (they didn't know how to use it). This [git repo by Jonathan Tremblay](https://github.com/JonathanTremblay/UnityExportToZip) of transforming a whole project into a zip for the students really helped a lot. For code updates, I would post unity package updates on a google classroom. They submitted their end project in a unity package in google classroom. Regarding prefabs the documentation and myself were very clear: there a bunch of prefabs they couldn't change directly over the risk of losing stuff. That part actually went well. 


> **The fourth goal was: whenever needed and possible, use Gen AI for 3d modelling. From my tests, I ended up using 2 models in the experience: a carnation and a white handkerchief.**

> The fifth goal came with development: at the end, students really wanted to combine their levels in a specific cadence/storyline. But they were out of availability after developing their levels. So I talked with my colleagues in the school on Creative Writing and based on screenshots and the game idea, one of her students wrote a story combining all these scenes. I then aggregate it, integrated it into the game and polished the scenes before releasing to the public.

## Development

### Step 1 - XR Simulator on Mac & Calibration

I started with the most complicated part - if there was no way of having a Simulator running on Mac and calibrating the experience once the headset was removed, then it wouldn't be a frictionless experience for everyone as I desired.

Calibration was really important. As mentioned, the user would walk through the levels without teleporting, as it was confided in a cage. Because of that, it would always need to start from a set position.

![Level design directive - wherever location you end in the physical space, it is where you start from the level after]({{ site.url }}{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/xr-bootcamp/xr-design-fellowship/module/08/level-design-diagram.png)

> :memo: **Main development Tip for this game level design:** Whichever location you end in the physical space, it is where you start from the level after.

In addition, as the cage dimensions were different (3m per 2.5m), the headset x-z measurements when starting/restarting the experience could invert sometimes. When these measures inverted, I had to rotate the environment 90 degrees in the Y axis to make sure every level did not look wrong.

The calibration took way longer than expected because Quest Link appeared to not update boundary position using Unity XR Toolkit. Plus, the event system for removing the headset in Unity XR Toolkit was not easy to follow. 
I asked in the XR Creators chat for some help and I got this reply which helped a lot (thank you so much endasil).

![Help from XR Creators on channel on calibration issue with Quest]({{ site.url }}{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/xr-bootcamp/xr-design-fellowship/module/08/dev-support-02-xr-creators.png)

Then I had another problem related with quest link. I didn't want to recenter based on the headset position, I wanted to recenter based on the floor/room dimensions (what is called stage). 
And it appears quest link ignores everything about turning off recentering. 

I almost gave up from the project until I tried in standalone mode and it was working, with **Allow Recenter off and Tracking Origin Stage.**

{% include video id="993956080" provider="vimeo" %}

> :memo: **Note:** I couldn't capture the restart of the museum experience by taking off the headset. It appears while recording or casting the Quest would never trigger the "headset off event". This had happened during the small showcase at the end, but I only discovered the actual bug when I was writing this article and making small clips.

I developed most of this initial part with Windows and Quest Link - it was handy to test it live so many times (except the recentering part). With Windows, I was managing pragmas to either use the XR simulator (like the students would do) or use the Quest Link. 

By the end of the project, I created a player prefab and 2 scriptable objects to manage this complexity and just switch the value depending on developing platform. It would ignore the simulator on windows and use quest link whenever possible; and on Mac use the simulator at all times. The player prefab would enable the simulator only and if only it should be used.

{% include video id="994277898" provider="vimeo" %}


### Step 2 - Create basic tutorial for students

{% include gallery id="gallery-dev-support" layout="half" caption="Some prefabs to help students designing their levels" %}

- Use grab interactable for to grab virtual objects, with audio cue when grabbing /dropping object (either with controllers for the simulator or hand tracking for real life testing). *Unity XR Toolkit only allowed pinch grabbing. I had to give up from other grabbing types and taught people and student to work that way*.
- Make semi transparent cage (2.5 by 3m) to help students and myself understand how much walking space the player had. When needed, implemented a tag definition to hide these type of objects when entering play mode.
- make basic prefabs for UI (Text, image, video, 360 video)
- async load with fade in / fade out
- On grabbing some special objects (they had a blue particle effect on them developed by my former colleague [Pedro Dias](https://www.linkedin.com/in/pedro-g-dias/)) they would open portal to either a new level or new location.
- Test Unity XR Toolkit ladders and climbing walls, adjust to game size
- Have 3 levels working as a small tutorial for students understand how all would work: 
  - calibration level: stand on circle with arrow, as starting location for the entire experience
  - intro: grab object and take it to portal to travel to next location
  - test level with multiple portals within the level, ladders and climbing walls for them to understand it is possible to create many things [3 x 2.5m].
- Make hands disappear when grabbing objects (it was glitchy when that didn't happen and people did not seem to "lose a limb" when grabbing)
- All of these features were already integrated in prefabs so that the students would just use drag & drop Unity events. For other things, used mostly Unity actions to hide that part in the code where they wouldn't touch.
- [Pedro Dias](https://www.linkedin.com/in/pedro-g-dias/) also gathered and made some nice 3D models for students to use in their levels.

> The first feedback I had from the initial experience was a 8 year old kid saying: "I am not sure if I prefer that digital world. But please put more climbing wall spheres - I am very tiny and it was scary" She was in awe with VR. Other colleagues that supported these students were very ecstatic.

### Step 3 - Documentation; pitch project to students; incorporate student feedback; Tutor, mentor, project manager

I wrote a GDD and session-by-session script in portuguese for them to understand what needed to be done and how. They are in portuguese but you can find them in [here](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1Aj5XmXlMycrWPSCeLwS6ia7tanMFYijB?usp=drive_link).

I presented this project to 16 teenagers across 3 different time schedules and set the challenge - for them to develop their own scenes for this experience on the topic of portuguese democracy in 2 weeks and a bit.

They provided some valuable user testing and feedback for: 
- improving the portals, 
- trying to improve the grab interaction (I managed slightly but it was still mostly pinched based).

### Step 5 - A Story was born; Aggregating and displaying to parents and students

As mentioned before, students wanted to combine their stories instead of having to go from the prison to a dream. 
To continue the idea of having a mostly student-led project, I asked my colleague [Maria Vieira](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mlvieiraart/), Workshop Leader on Creative Writing, if she knew a student or two that could be interested in writing a story for the game based on the levels already made. 

> The students who had developed the story were no longer to continue working on the project, so the creative writers would have to adapt to what was already in place. And they sure did!

With [Samuel Peruzzolo](https://www.linkedin.com/in/samuelperuzzolo/), workshop leader in music, he recorded the student writer as narrator in our studio.

Then I had over a little bit more than a weekend to: 
- aggregate every scene on different computer and projects, 
- include the audio tracks, 
- reduce poly count in some assets using Blender decimate modifier (like the pillows in the dream scene)
- make a few extra scripts to delay action until ... (narrator had finished); and continue background music through levels (some students had made a funny animation of a radio that should be playing the Portuguese song for freedom after a certain part) 
- used blender to decrease some student level poly counts (by decimating many 3d models)
- rearranged some unfinished levels from the students... 
- ...and yeah tested endlessly and it was done.

## Next steps

I may reuse this project template for other workshops with more time and let students make small games/themes. But next time, I will do video tutorials (students are not used to text and image reports).

## Conclusion

It was one hell of a project. Despite trying to reduce the scope at its core, game dev is always hard when you are not going on the standard route and away from standard tutorials. A big example of this was when trying to simplify the experience for end users (to just walking and grab without teleportation), it made the experience harder from a development standpoint because it is not standard at all. 

When it came to working with teenagers, creating prefabs and a structure so that students from multiple teams could work async and without git, was quite complicated but again, also very interesting. 

Another good point was to manage their expectations. It was super hard - now we have all this mindset that anything can be easily done when the reality of game development is much more complex. 

At the end, it was a great learning experience for me and the students. I am deeply proud of what we were able to accomplish and learn in the process.

To end, thank you [Gabriele Romagnoli](https://www.linkedin.com/in/gabriele-romagnolixr/) for inspiration, [TUMO](https://tumo.pt/) for letting me try doing this project, and [XR Bootcamp](https://xrbootcamp.com/) for also letting me do this and continuous support throughout the year.
